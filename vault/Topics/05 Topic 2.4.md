# Topic 2.4: Context of Philippine governance

> [!plain] In plain words
> you can't understand Philippine governance by looking only at formal structures. Filipino social life existed long before the modern state, and history, identity, culture, regional diversity, and values all shape how government works today.

**Why this topic matters:** Filipino social organization (kinship, alliances, rituals, community, social obligations) existed **before** the modern state. Institutions didn't suddenly appear in 1987.

**Readings in order:** (1) Ayala Museum, (2) Niels Mulder, (3) Raul de Guzman, (4) Filipino Story Studio, (5) FEATR, (6) J.W. Diokno's letter.

## 2.4.1 Ayala Museum: "How Different were Early Filipino Traditions?"

- Life is marked by **3 stages: birth (life), marriage or alliance, death.** Human experience is both **universal and cultural**.
- Early Filipinos slept on palm-leaf mats; houses were on **stilts** (protection from drafts, dirt, pests), north and south.
- Skilled **weavers** (abundant fibers) and **metalworkers** (brass, bronze, gold).
- **Weddings** were community affairs; the man took the woman's name; **divorce** existed, with property split by who was at fault.
- Regardless of belief, they believed in an **afterlife**.

## 2.4.2 Niels Mulder: "Filipino Identity: The Haunting Question"

- **Big question:** what does it mean to be Filipino, and why does PH governance look the way it does?
- He links identity to Spanish colonial history, American imperialism, cultural colonization, Southeast Asian social organization, and nationalism.
- **Central concern:** the **gap between the state (public sphere) and people's everyday world**. Systemic divides make it hard for Filipinos to identify with the collective whole and become a nation of committed citizens.
- **Family-centered** life (bilateral kinship, debts of gratitude) competes with loyalty to the larger political community.
- **Chain:** history → culture/identity → social relations → political behavior → governance.

## 2.4.3 Raul P. de Guzman: "Is There a Philippine Public Administration?" (1986)

- **Answer: yes**, as much as there is a French, American, or Japanese PA. But the question must be defined properly.
- **"Public administration" has 3 meanings:**
  1. Government in action / the [[Bureaucracy (Weber)|bureaucracy]]
  2. The field of study
  3. The profession of managing government affairs
- **The real PA 102 question:** what makes PH PA **"Philippine"**? De Guzman asks whether our bureaucracy has **structural** and **behavioral** characteristics that are uniquely Filipino, versus traits shared with bureaucracies elsewhere.
- PA is shaped by its **social, cultural, economic, and political environment**. It's not a technical machine imported from abroad, so you can't just say "Singapore does X, so let's copy X."
- PH PA is built on values like [[Utang na Loob|utang na loob]] and bayanihan, and the field has changed and grown quickly.
- **Indigenous governance example:** **Gawad Kalinga**, a partnership of **government, business, and civil society**. Good governance rests on that three-way partnership.

## 2.4.4 The Filipino Story Studio: "Why Is the Philippines Broken Today?"

- A **popular historical narrative**, not an academic theory.
- Early Filipinos lived by 3 core values: **kapwa** (shared self, equalness), **bayanihan** (togetherness), **kabutihang loob** (kindness).
- Because of these, women held high places, people prospered, leaders were expected to rule fairly, and guests felt so welcome some stayed.
- **What changed:**
  - **Spain (16th century):** slavery and forced assimilation → colonial mentality; destroyed equalness.
  - **America** (bought the PH from Spain): more assimilation and **individualism** (own needs first).
  - **Japan:** further distorted the people.
- About **500 years** of colonization and invasion distorted our identity.
- **Safe academic framing:** present problems should be understood through **historical development**, not as isolated modern problems.

## 2.4.5 FEATR: "How Dangerous is Mindanao?"

- Mindanao has long been home to Muslim culture, with rich food, culture, and beauty.
- **[[Autonomy (CAR vs BARMM)|ARMM]]** areas suffered centuries of socio-political violence; Sulu has long fought for independence.
- International bad news about the PH (before the EJK era) was mostly terrorism and kidnappings in ARMM provinces.
- **Message:** don't let the negative **1%** define the great **99%**.
- **PA link:** regional diversity, conflict, security, **center-periphery relations**, national narratives vs. local realities. Negative images feed region-based political identities.
- **PA question:** can one governance model fit very different local contexts?

## 2.4.6 J.W. Diokno: Letter to his son Popoy

- Written in **detention in 1972** to his son Jose Ramon, about whether studying law was worth it under [[Martial Law|martial law]].
- Themes: the importance of **law even under authoritarian rule**, justice, truth, service, rule of law, and **lawyers' role in rebuilding society**.
- Key line: **"Government is always and only an instrument of the people."**
- **PA shift:** from "What institutions exist?" to **"What values should guide the people working inside them?"** and "What kind of citizen or public servant should a democracy produce?"

## Core idea of 2.4

Philippine governance is not produced by institutions alone: **history + identity + culture + regional diversity + social relationships + values → Philippine governance.**
