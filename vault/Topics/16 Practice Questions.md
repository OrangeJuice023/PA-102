# Likely exam questions

Comprehensive active-recall practice bank aligned with Sir LTD's midterm exam specifications: 60% individual written exam (multiple choice, identification, fill in the blanks, distinctions, scenarios, doctrines), 40% team Jeopardy quiz game (5 categories × 5 point values from 100 to 500 points), essay and synthesis prompts, and multi-theme Final Jeopardy questions.

## Written exam practice questions

Practice traditional written-exam retrieval across all covered topics. Try answering each question from memory before checking the answer key.

### Questions

1. **Multiple choice.** [2.1–2.2] Which case held that the [[Filipino First Policy]] in Art. XII, Sec. 10 is self-executing? (a) Tañada v. Angara (b) Manila Prince Hotel v. GSIS (c) Angara v. Electoral Commission (d) Planas v. Gil

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **(b)** Manila Prince Hotel v. GSIS (1997).

2. **Identification.** [2.1–2.2] The government functions that "constitute the very bonds of society" and are compulsory.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Constituent** functions.

3. **Identification.** [2.1–2.2] The kind of government function where the State "strips away its sovereign mantle" and can be held liable like a private company.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Proprietary** functions.

4. **Fill in the blanks.** [2.1–2.2] In Angara v. Electoral Commission, [[Separation of Powers|separation of powers]] is not an express provision. It comes from the division of powers in Articles ____, ____, and ____ of the Constitution.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **VI** (Legislative), **VII** (Executive), **VIII** (Judicial).

5. **Identification.** [2.1–2.2] The case stating that an unconstitutional law "confers no rights; it imposes no duties; it affords no protection; it creates no office."

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Sameer Overseas v. Cabiles** (2014).

6. **Distinguish.** [3.2] Residencia vs. Visita.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Residencia** = investigation of an official's conduct **after** the term. **Visita** = investigation **during** the term, by a Visitador-General. Both could lead to fines, dismissal, prison, or seizure of property.

7. **Short essay.** [3.1] Explain: "No nation-state does not mean no government" in precolonial Philippines. Give at least two pieces of evidence.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> There were political communities with leaders (datu, rajah, sultan), **customary law** that even bound the datu, councils of elders, specialized roles (baylan, umalohokan), trade and tribute ties (Laguna Copperplate, 900 CE; Butuan's 1001 tribute mission), and alliances. Jocano even calls the barangay a State; Junker calls it a pre-state chiefdom. Fragmentation ≠ absence.

8. **Distinguish.** [3.2] La Liga Filipina vs. the Katipunan (goal and nature).

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **La Liga (1892, Rizal):** reformist, not armed; wanted equality and representation **within** Spain. **Katipunan (1892):** revolutionary; wanted **independence**; also a political and moral organization (Kartilya).

9. **Short essay.** [2.3] When does [[Utang na Loob|utang na loob]] help governance, and when does it hurt it? Use Cleofas and De Joya et al.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Helps:** gratitude, reciprocity, solidarity, when practiced **with justice (katarungan)** for the common good (Cleofas). **Hurts:** when it becomes favoritism, [[Patronage|patronage]], or the basis for voting (gifts at kasal, binyag, libing). De Joya: most voters rejected it as a voting basis, except Class E.

10. **Identification.** [2.5] The problem a frontline public servant faces when applying a general rule to many different real cases with limited resources.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The **street-level dilemma.**

11. **Identification.** [Oct 3] The power to alter, modify, nullify, or set aside what a subordinate officer has done and substitute the President's judgment for theirs.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Control.**

12. **Short answer.** [Oct 3] What is the doctrine of qualified political agency? Give its three limits.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> Department secretaries are the President's **alter egos**; their acts within their authority, unless disapproved, are presumed acts of the President. **Limits:** the President keeps the power to confirm, modify, or reverse; cannot delegate functions the Constitution or law requires the President to do personally; cannot control independent constitutional commissions in their protected functions.

13. **Fill in the blanks.** [Oct 3] In cases of ____ or ____, when public safety requires it, the President may suspend the privilege of the writ of habeas corpus or declare [[Martial Law|martial law]] for not more than ____ days, subject to ____ review and ____ review.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Invasion; rebellion; 60; congressional; judicial.**

14. **Fill in the blanks.** [Oct 3] (a) The President submits the proposed budget within ____ days from the opening of every regular session. (b) A veto is overridden by ____ of all members of each House, voting ____. (c) Treaties need concurrence of at least ____ of all Senate members. (d) Midnight appointments are banned from ____ months before the presidential election until the end of the term.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> (a) **30** (b) **two-thirds; separately** (c) **two-thirds** (d) **two**

15. **Distinguish.** [Oct 3] The President's power of control vs. reorganization authority. Give one limit on reorganization.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Control** = revising a subordinate's **acts**. **Reorganization** = changing the **structure, location, powers, and functions** of offices. **Limits:** must be backed by the Constitution, a statute, or valid continuing authority; cannot be used to remove an officer in violation of **security of tenure**; cannot go beyond the authority given by law.

16. **Identification.** [Foundational] The administrative doctrine and historical belief that politics (deciding policy) and administration (carrying it out) should be kept strictly separate.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The **politics-administration dichotomy.**

17. **Short answer.** [Foundational] According to Dwight Waldo, public administration must not be guided solely by the "3 Es." What are the 3 Es, and what else does PA require?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The 3 Es are **efficiency, economy, and effectiveness.** Waldo argued that PA cannot be purely technical; it also requires a **pyramid or pinnacle of values** (justice, equity, human dignity) to guide policy and administrative action.

18. **Multiple choice.** [Foundational] What are the four essential elements of the State (PTGS)? (a) People, Territory, Government, [[Sovereignty]] (b) Population, Terrain, Governance, Supremacy (c) Public, Treaty, Government, Security (d) People, Tax, Gendarmerie, Sovereignty

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **(a)** People, Territory, Government, Sovereignty (PTGS).

19. **Distinguish.** [Foundational] A democratic state vs. a republican state as framed in the PA 102 foundational lecture.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Democratic state:** centers on the direct or representative exercise of popular power (representatives acting as conduits, agents, or trustees), risking bandwagoning or mob rule. **Republican state:** centers on institutional survival, territorial defense, a strong military, and a strong economy against external invaders and threats.

20. **Case doctrine.** [Foundational] Which case established that the State is an "ideal person"—intangible, invisible, and able to act only through the instrumentality of its government?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Poindexter v. Greenhow** (1885).

21. **Identification.** [Foundational] Government is defined as an ensemble of political institutions that impose a mandate and rule on people in a territory. What two fundamental properties make up "political institutions"?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Agency** (the legal and practical capacity to act) and **Normativity** (the established norms, rules, and behavioral standards that guide action).

22. **Distinguish.** [Foundational] Inquisitorial adjudication vs. Adversarial adjudication.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Inquisitorial:** the judge takes an active, direct investigative role in discovering facts and examining evidence. **Adversarial:** an impartial, neutral judge hears competing arguments and evidence presented by opposing parties.

23. **Multiple choice.** [Foundational] What are the three recognized forms of a [[De Facto Government|de facto]] government? (a) Legal sense, paramount force, and revolt/insurrection (b) Constitutional, monarchical, and dictatorial (c) Parliamentary, presidential, and federal (d) Civil, military, and ecclesiastical

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **(a)** Legal sense (possession of territory and formal state machinery), paramount force (an invading military force overwhelming native defenders), and revolt/insurrection (an internal armed movement displacing the established order).

24. **Enumeration.** [Foundational] Name at least four of the six classic features of Weberian rational-legal [[Bureaucracy (Weber)|bureaucracy]].

> [!answer]- Show answer
> Any four of: (1) **specialization / division of labor**; (2) **hierarchical authority**; (3) **formal rational-legal rules**; (4) **impersonality**; (5) **merit and fitness** in recruitment; (6) **security of tenure**.

25. **Distinguish.** [Foundational] Policy capacity vs. State capacity.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Policy capacity** = the ability of the state to formulate coherent strategies and successfully achieve its intended policy goals. **State capacity** = whether the state actually possesses the administrative machinery, fiscal resources, and personnel to execute those policies.

26. **Distinguish.** [Foundational] Bureaucratic polity vs. Proprietary polity.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Bureaucratic polity** = a governance system dominated by a technocratic and military/civil elite insulated from social pressure. **Proprietary polity** = a system characterized by weak state institutions where powerful economic and resource elites dictate state decisions and capture public resources.

27. **Enumeration.** [Topic 2] What are the four ways of understanding an organization or government discussed in the introductory framework?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> (1) **What is written down** (formal laws, codes, jurisprudence); (2) **What is believed to exist** (popular perception, public trust/cynicism); (3) **What actually exists** (real org charts, actual workflows, informal practices); (4) **What is actually needed** (diagnosed institutional reforms based on societal values).

28. **Short answer.** [Topic 2] According to Casis ("If I Were to Reform Philippine Society"), what was the "fused society" of precolonial Philippines, and why did borrowing separation of powers produce unintended problems?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> In precolonial polities, all powers (executive, judicial, legislative, spiritual, economic) were **fused** in single leaders (Lakan, Datu, Sultan). When the Philippines borrowed formal Western separation of powers without integrating indigenous relational values (such as bayanihan and utang na loob), the formal institutions were subverted by informal patronage and corruption.

29. **Identification.** [Topic 2] The constitutional economic policy, originally initiated under President Carlos P. Garcia and now embodied in Art. XII, Sec. 10 of the 1987 Constitution, giving preference to qualified Filipinos in the national economy.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The **Filipino First Policy** (Art. XII, Sec. 10).

30. **Scenario/application.** [Topic 2] In "The Decapitalization of the Philippines," how does Tiglao demonstrate that foreign conglomerates evade the 60% Filipino ownership requirement in strategic public utilities like telecommunications?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> By issuing non-voting or **voting preferred shares** with minimal equity value to domestic front-men while foreign corporate investors retain controlling beneficial equity and extract billions in profits overseas (e.g., via Hong Kong holding firms).

31. **Short answer.** [Topic 2] Define the fundamental interaction: Formal institutions + [[Informal Institutions|Informal institutions]] → Actual behavior → Governance.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> Formal rules set the legal framework, but informal institutions (cultural values, personal debts, patron-client ties) interact with those rules to determine the actual day-to-day behavior of public officials, which ultimately produces the governance outcome.

32. **Fill in the blanks.** [2.1–2.2] In the 1987 Philippine Constitution, the Legislative Department is established in Article ____, the Executive Department in Article ____, and the Judicial Department in Article ____.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Article VI** (Legislative), **Article VII** (Executive), and **Article VIII** (Judicial).

33. **Identification.** [2.1–2.2] The doctrine that the Constitution is the highest law of the land, requiring all statutes, treaties, executive orders, and administrative rules to conform to it.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **[[Constitutional Supremacy|Constitutional supremacy]].**

34. **Enumeration.** [2.1–2.2] What is the legal hierarchy of laws below the 1987 Constitution?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Constitution → Statutes / Acts of Congress → Administrative rules, executive orders, and municipal ordinances.** Lower rules cannot contradict or exceed higher rules.

35. **Distinguish.** [2.1–2.2] Self-executing constitutional provisions vs. Non-self-executing constitutional provisions.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Self-executing:** provisions that are immediately enforceable and judicially actionable on their own without requiring implementing legislation (e.g., Art. XII, Sec. 10 per Manila Prince Hotel). **Non-self-executing:** broad statements of principle and policy that require statutory enactments to create judicially enforceable rights (e.g., Art. II provisions per Tañada v. Angara).

36. **Case doctrine.** [2.1–2.2] In Tañada v. Angara (1997), how did the Supreme Court rule on the nature of Article II (Declaration of Principles and State Policies) regarding the WTO Agreement?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The Court held that the economic provisions in Article II are **non-self-executing** principles and guidelines for the legislature, not judicially enforceable rights that can nullify international treaties like the WTO Agreement.

37. **Enumeration.** [2.1–2.2] Name at least four of the eight constituent functions of government identified in Philippine jurisprudence.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> Any four of: (1) keeping public order and protecting persons and property; (2) fixing domestic relations (marriage, parents-children); (3) regulating property holding, transfer, and contract rights; (4) determining contract rights; (5) defining and punishing crimes; (6) administering civil justice; (7) determining political duties and citizenship; (8) conducting foreign relations and defense.

38. **Distinguish.** [2.1–2.2] Governmental functions vs. Proprietary functions in terms of state liability and sovereign immunity.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Governmental:** performed in the exercise of sovereign public authority and general welfare; the State enjoys **immunity from suit** unless it expressly consents. **Proprietary:** performed for private, corporate, or commercial benefit; the State **strips away its sovereign mantle** and is liable for damages and contracts just like a private business.

39. **Case doctrine.** [2.1–2.2] What rule did the Supreme Court emphasize in MERALCO v. City of Muntinlupa (2021) regarding administrative rulemaking and municipal tax powers?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **"You cannot rise above your source."** Administrative regulations and local tax ordinances cannot exceed or modify the statutory authority granted by the enabling legislation.

40. **Identification.** [2.1–2.2] Match the law: (a) RA 3019, (b) RA 6713, and (c) RA 7080.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> (a) **RA 3019:** Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act (1960). (b) **RA 6713:** Code of Conduct and Ethical Standards for Public Officials and Employees (1989). (c) **RA 7080:** Plunder Law (1991).

41. **Identification.** [2.3] An unwritten, socially established rule, norm, relationship, or practice that shapes political and administrative behavior outside formal law.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> An **informal institution.**

42. **Short answer.** [2.3] According to Cleofas, what essential principle must accompany utang na loob to keep it from degenerating into nepotism, corruption, and favoritism?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Katarungan (justice)**—specifically the fair and equitable distribution of resources and opportunities for the common good, ensuring that personal gratitude does not override fairness to the public.

43. **Scenario/application.** [2.3] In the study by De Joya et al. (2024) on voting in Cavite, why did Class E respondents view politicians' gifts at kasal, binyag, and libing as an acceptable basis for voting, while higher income classes rejected it?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> Class E families experience extreme economic vulnerability and lack access to dependable basic social services; for them, immediate material assistance during crises (death, illness, baptism) fulfills life-saving survival needs that formal government programs fail to deliver promptly.

44. **Distinguish.** [2.3] Morales's view of [[Diskarte|diskarte]] vs. Hilotin's critique of diskarte.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Morales:** diskarte is creative, reactive, and adaptive problem-solving within real situational constraints and inequality. **Hilotin:** diskarte functions as a cultural excuse for impunity, rule-bending, fixers, and petty corruption.

45. **Short answer.** [2.3] According to Ibe, how does the informal institution of [[Pakikisama|pakikisama]] and the concept of an "extended political family" contribute to political dynasties?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> Pakikisama demands maintaining interpersonal harmony and personal loyalty above all else; when transposed into public office, officials treat relatives and political patrons as an "extended political family," shielding them from accountability and endorsing family dynasties across generations.

46. **Identification.** [2.3] Mendoza (2025) analyzes concepts like [[Hiya|hiya]], tino, and bait not as fixed psychological traits, but as what kind of communicative lenses through which Filipinos judge political scandals?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Vernacular grammars**—indigenous linguistic and cultural frameworks through which everyday citizens interpret, debate, and moralize public accountability and political scandals.

47. **Short answer.** [2.4] What material and social evidence from the Ayala Museum collection proves that early Philippine communities had sophisticated governance and social order prior to Spanish colonization?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The presence of raised stilt-house architecture, advanced metallurgical and gold craftsmanship, complex woven textiles, community-regulated marriage and matrilocal naming customs, and established property and divorce laws.

48. **Short answer.** [2.4] What does Niels Mulder describe as the central problem in "Filipino Identity: The Haunting Question"?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The deep **gap between the state (the public civic sphere) and the citizen's everyday world**, where primordial loyalties to bilateral family kinship and personal networks consistently overpower commitment to the abstract nation-state.

49. **Enumeration.** [2.4] According to Raul P. de Guzman (1986), what are the three distinct meanings of "Public Administration"?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> (1) **Government in action** (the operational bureaucracy); (2) **The field of academic study / discipline**; (3) **The professional practice / vocation** of managing public affairs.

50. **Identification.** [2.4] In his 1972 detention letter to his son Popoy, what foundational principle did Senator Jose W. Diokno articulate regarding the ultimate role of government?

> [!answer]- Show answer

51. **Identification.** [2.5] According to Pulse Asia surveys reported by Baroña (2026), what are the top two concrete, immediate demands of Filipino citizens?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Lower food prices / affordable food** and **job creation / livelihood.**

52. **Scenario/application.** [2.5] Describe the "street-level dilemma" faced by a frontline government social worker dealing with 100 applicants and a budget for only 40.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The administrator must implement a rigid formal rule ("first-come, first-served") across 100 equally deserving, impoverished citizens while possessing funds for only 40, forcing discretion at the point of delivery where formal rules clash directly with human empathy and informal pressures.

53. **Distinguish.** [2.5] A political party vs. a political faction as defined by Dulay & Arguelles (2026).

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Party:** an institutionalized organization built upon a stable political program, ideology, and collective identity. **Faction:** an informal, fluid coalition organized around prominent personalities, patronage resources, and personalistic alliances that cut across party labels.

54. **Multiple choice.** [2.5] According to Dulay & Arguelles (2026), what is the strongest demographic and geographic divide among Filipino voters? (a) Religion (b) Geographic region (Luzon / Visayas / Mindanao) (c) Left-Right ideological spectrum (d) Social class alone

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **(b)** Geographic region (e.g., Marcos dominating the rest of Luzon, Duterte dominating Mindanao, and the Opposition retaining strength in parts of the Visayas).

55. **Distinguish.** [3.1] Burling's distinction between "hills" terrain and "plains" terrain in Southeast Asian political organization.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Hills:** rugged terrain, lower population density, swidden/shifting cultivation, indigenous tribal religions, small segmented communities with strong local [[Autonomy (CAR vs BARMM)|autonomy]] resistant to outside conquest. **Plains:** flat riverine land, high density, intensive wet-rice agriculture, world religions, large integrated political settlements.

56. **Distinguish.** [3.1] Cognatic (bilateral) kinship vs. Fictive kinship in early Philippine social organization.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Cognatic kinship:** bilateral descent traced simultaneously through both mother and father. **Fictive kinship:** ritually created, non-blood alliances (such as the sanduguan blood oath or compadrazgo) establishing mutual obligations of loyalty and defense.

57. **Distinguish.** [3.1] F. Landa Jocano's view of the precolonial barangay vs. Laura Lee Junker's view.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Jocano:** views the barangay as functionally a sovereign **State** with specialized offices, social stratification, legal order, and external diplomacy. **Junker:** cautions that it was a **pre-state complex society / chiefdom**, marked by hereditary elites and segmentary, shifting alliances rather than a [[Centralization|centralized]] state apparatus.

58. **Identification.** [3.1] In precolonial barangays, who was the spiritual and healing authority who could be either a woman or a queer man?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The **Baylan** (or Catalonan).

59. **Short answer.** [3.1] Give two examples of customary law that demonstrate that a precolonial datu's authority was not unlimited.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> Any two of: (1) mandatory gold or property restitution for unlawful destruction of property; (2) customary fines for theft; (3) reciprocal sanctions or blood-money fines for killing a free person; (4) the requirement to consult the Council of Elders on major community disputes.

60. **Identification.** [3.1] The Malay concept discussed by Talib describing the world as an integrated "water-land" or liquid space connecting maritime societies.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Tanah air** (literally "land-water"), viewing maritime spaces not as barriers but as connecting commercial and political highways.

61. **Enumeration.** [3.2] List the administrative hierarchy of Spanish colonial governance in the Philippines from central to barrio levels.

62. **Identification.** [3.2] The privilege granted to the Spanish Alcalde Mayor permitting him to engage in commercial trade within his province despite his official administrative duties.

63. **Distinguish.** [3.2] The Spanish legal dualism under the Recopilación de Leyes de las Indias: República de Españoles vs. República de Indios.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **República de Españoles:** the legal, fiscal, and social order governing Spaniards, governed by metropolitan Spanish law. **República de Indios:** the separate, subordinated administrative legal order governing native Filipinos under communal tribute, labor extraction, and paternalistic colonial codes.

64. **Short answer.** [3.2] What was the central governance contradiction produced by the Spanish Cádiz Constitution of 1812 in the Philippines?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> It promised **constitutional equality and representation in the Spanish Cortes on paper**, while maintaining rigid racial, economic, and administrative colonial subordination and friarocracy in actual practice.

65. **Short answer.** [3.2] According to John Schumacher, what was the true historical role of Rizal's Noli Me Tangere in the Philippine Revolution?

> [!answer]- Show answer

67. **Identification.** [3.2] The Cavite assembly on March 22, 1897 that dissolved the Katipunan's authority and elected a central revolutionary government, sparking a legitimacy crisis between Bonifacio and Tirona.

68. **Short answer.** [3.2] In the 1899 Malolos Constitution, what specific provision allowed Aguinaldo to issue executive decrees while Congress was closed during wartime?

> [!answer]- Show answer

77. **Short answer.** [3.4] Why was the Japanese-sponsored Second Republic considered merely a de facto government by paramount force rather than a de jure sovereign government?

> [!answer]- Show answer

78. **Short answer.** [3.5] What is Pacifico Agabin's core thesis in "The Birth of the [[Mestizo Legal System|Mestizo]]"?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The Philippine legal system is a **hybrid ("mestizo") system** combining a Spanish civil law foundation (private, family, property, and penal law), an Anglo-American common law and constitutional overlay (public law, [[Police Power|police power]], [[Due Process|due process]], [[Judicial Review|judicial review]], commercial law), and surviving indigenous/customary law.

79. **Identification.** [3.5] The constitutional power of the State to regulate private conduct, property, and business for public health, safety, morals, and general welfare.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Police power.**

80. **Case doctrine.** [3.5] In De Villata v. Stanley, what constitutional tension was highlighted regarding government regulation of private shipping vessels?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The clash between the state's **police power** to compel private shipping lines to carry government mail for public service and the private owners' property rights protected by constitutional **due process**.

81. **Distinguish.** [3.5] Procedural due process vs. Substantive due process.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Procedural due process:** whether the government complied with fair notice, hearing, and established lawful procedure. **Substantive due process:** whether the governmental enactment or interference itself is arbitrary, unreasonable, or oppressive, regardless of procedure.

82. **Short answer.** [3.5] How did American colonization alter criminal and civil procedure from the Spanish civil law tradition?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> It replaced the Spanish inquisitorial criminal procedure with the American **adversarial/accusatorial** procedure (General Order No. 58), instituted evidentiary rules including the exclusionary rule, and introduced common-law doctrines of precedent and judicial review.

83. **Identification.** [3.6] What term describes the condition under the Marcos regime where a centralized Filipino government concentrated authoritarian power over its own people, mirroring colonial extraction?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Internal colonization.**

84. **Short answer.** [3.6] How was the 1973 Constitution declared ratified under Presidential Proclamation No. 1102?

> [!answer]- Show answer

93. **Case doctrine.** [4.1] What doctrine did the Supreme Court establish in Marcos v. Manglapus (1989) regarding presidential powers?

> [!answer]- Show answer

94. **Short answer.** [Oct 3] Under the 1987 Constitution, what are the strict limitations placed on the President's power to declare martial law or suspend the writ of habeas corpus?

> [!answer]- Show answer

95. **Short answer.** [Oct 3] What is the constitutional rule on "midnight appointments" under Art. VII, Sec. 15, and what are its two narrow exceptions?

96. **Short answer.** [Oct 3] As Commander-in-Chief, what specific constitutional powers does the President hold to respond to lawless violence, invasion, and rebellion, and what is the maximum duration of any resulting martial law or habeas corpus suspension?

> [!answer]- Show answer

97. **Short answer.** [Oct 3] Under what conditions may Congress delegate [[Emergency Powers|emergency powers]] to the President, and what may the State temporarily do to privately owned public utilities during a declared national emergency?

> [!answer]- Show answer

During a national emergency, when the public interest requires, the State may temporarily take over or direct the operation of privately owned public utilities or businesses affected with public interest.

99. **Short answer.** [Oct 3] What are the statutory sources of the President's reorganization authority, and what are three legal limits on that authority?

100. **Short answer.** [4.1] According to Remigio Agpalo, what are the three regime types (parliamentary, presidential, and pangulo), their respective sites of supremacy, and the core value animating each?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> **Parliamentary** (Westminster/UK): supremacy resides in the **legislature**; core value = **liberty** (democracy). **Presidential** (US): supremacy is **balanced** through separation of powers, executive equal to legislative; core value = **equality**. **Pangulo** (PH): supremacy resides in the **executive**; core value = **fraternity / pagdamay**.

101. **Short answer.** [4.1] Why is the Philippine presidency described as a "Hyper Pangulo" regime? Name at least four of the distinct bureaucracies that all ultimately answer to the President.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The presidency is "hyper" because **many distinct bureaucracies all ultimately answer to the President**, combined with executive supremacy rooted in pagdamay, emergency and residual powers, immunity from suit, symbolic status, and appointing power over co-equal bodies ("madaming butas kaya hyper pangulo"). At least four of these bureaucracies: (1) each branch's own bureaucracy; (2) independent constitutional bodies; (3) state finance (fiscal and monetary); (4) public corporations (LGUs and GOCCs); (5) national security and the separate military bureaucracy; (6) law enforcement (PNP, SAF, Coast Guard); (7) regional autonomous and administrative regions; (8) intergovernmental bureaucracies.

102. **Short answer.** [4.1] What is the doctrine of presidential immunity from suit during incumbency, what does it cover and not cover, and what are two workarounds or remaining accountability remedies?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The President **cannot be sued during incumbency**; the doctrine originates from the old idea that "the king can do no wrong." Suits are **deferred, not erased** (they can pile up). It covers **liabilities only, not accountability**. Workaround: file the case against the **Executive Secretary**. Remaining accountability remedies: **habeas corpus, amparo, and habeas data**.

103. **Short answer.** [4.1] How does the Philippine Vice President differ from the U.S. Vice President in terms of appointability and actual institutional function?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The Philippine Vice President **may be appointed to any office or department and do real work** — a genuine institutional role. In the U.S., the VP is a ceremonial **"spare tire"** and the butt of jokes. The PH VP is also a **fiscalizing** office and an **institutional evolution**, and what the VP does depends on the **President's discretion**.

104. **Enumeration.** [3.2] What is the "Kartilya ng Katipunan," and what is its core formula linking personal morality, social equality, and national liberation?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The **Kartilya ng Katipunan** was the Katipunan's moral and political code. Its core formula: **personal morality → social equality → national liberation**. It taught equality, brotherhood, defense of the oppressed, good character, duty, respect for women, and equality regardless of status or race.

> [!answer]- Show answer

106. **Short answer.** [3.6] Describe the institutional structure of government under the 1973 Constitution during the Marcos era, including the roles of the President and Prime Minister and the composition of the legislature.

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The 1973 Constitution moved toward a **parliamentary** government: the **President** was head of state and the **Prime Minister** was head of government; the legislature was a **unicameral Batasang Pambansa** (no Senate), whose members were called mambabatas pambansa. In practice, however, presidential decrees meant **executive and legislative power sat in one office**.

107. **Short answer.** [3.6] What are three distinctive features of the 1987 Constitution that set it apart as a reaction to authoritarian rule, and what is Sir's critique of these features?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> Distinctive 1987 features: (1) branded the **"charter of positive liberties"** — the only PH constitution to mention **love**; (2) directly addresses **separation of church and state**; (3) **empowers the judiciary** against the other branches (a shift **from legislative to judicial supremacy**). Sir's critique: **too many fail-safes restrict us** — for example, martial law cannot be declared even with China in our seas, only upon actual invasion (landfall), which may be too late.

108. **Short answer.** [2.4] According to the Filipino Story Studio narrative, what were the three core values of early Filipino society, and how did roughly 500 years of colonization distort them?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The three core values were **kapwa** (shared self, equalness), **bayanihan** (togetherness), and **kabutihang loob** (kindness). Under these values women held high places, leaders were expected to rule fairly, and guests felt welcome. Roughly **500 years of colonization and invasion** — Spanish slavery and forced assimilation, American individualism, and Japanese distortion — eroded these values and distorted Filipino identity.

109. **Short answer.** [2.5] According to Dulay & Arguelles (2026), what are the four major voter factions and their approximate shares, and what is the strongest demographic divide among Filipino voters?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The four factions and shares: **Duterte 37.8%**, **Opposition 26.4%**, **None 21.2%**, **Marcos 14.5%**. The strongest demographic divide is **geographic region**: Marcos dominates the Rest of Luzon, Duterte dominates Mindanao, and the Opposition retains strength in parts of the Visayas.

110. **Short answer.** [2.3] Describe the patron-client (parientes-padrinos) relationship in Philippine politics. At what life-cycle events does it typically activate?

> [!answer]- Show answer
> The **patron-client (parientes-padrinos)** relationship: a patron gives resources, access, and help, and the client feels gratitude, obligation, and loyalty, which circles back to the patron. It typically activates at life-cycle events: **kasal** (wedding), **binyag** (baptism), and **libing** (funeral), as well as fiestas.

### Final Jeopardy 1 — The Hyper Pangulo Formula: Law, History, and Informal Institutions

### Final Jeopardy 2 — Formal Rules vs. Informal Institutions in Administrative Reform

> [!answer]- Show answer
