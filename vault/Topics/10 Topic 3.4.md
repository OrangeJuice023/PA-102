> [!plain] In plain words
> Britain took Manila easily but held little else. Under Japan, Filipino officials worked with the occupiers on Quezon's orders but never swore allegiance, so the Philippines never lost its [[Sovereignty|sovereignty]]. Japan was only a **[[De Facto Government|de facto]]** government.

## British occupation (Seven Years' War)

- The Seven Years' War brought Britain to the PH. The British **captured Manila and Cavite** easily.
- Spain wasn't ready: the British warships were mistaken for **trade ships** (the Royal Philippine Company traded with Britain), and Spain's **weapons were made and kept in Cavite**, so Spain could hardly fight back.

## Japanese occupation

**The invasion:**

- Japanese immigrants in Manila were **sleeper agents** for about a decade, so Manila fell quickly, surrounded from the inside.
- Japanese planes bombed **Davao, Cebu, Iloilo, then Manila**, surprising the Americans. Galindez notes a massacre of people at a fiesta near the Welcome Rotonda in QC.
- The Americans fled to **Corregidor**, then Australia. **Quezon** was already at Corregidor and left with the government in exile.
- **Jorge B. Vargas** and **José Abad Santos** were left behind to deal with the Japanese. **Abad Santos** (Supreme Court Chief Justice) was taken with his family to Mindanao and **executed** for refusing to bow to the imperial flag.

**What Japan did:**

- **Abolished the Philippine legislature** (the building is now the National Museum) and **suspended the 1935 Constitution**.
- **Central Executive Commission:** told to **draft a new constitution (1943)**.
- **1943 Constitution:** meant to **de-Americanize** and "give Filipinos their independence." Japan wanted the PH to fight the Americans, framing itself as liberating Asia from colonial powers. But it ended up much like the 1935 Constitution, reusing parts that still worked while staying acceptable to Japan.
- **KALIBAPI** (Kapisanan sa Paglilingkod sa Bagong Pilipinas) was tasked to serve as the **Preparatory Commission for Philippine Independence**.

> [!warning] Check
> The **1943 Constitution** was used by the **Second Republic** under José P. Laurel and took effect with the Republic's inauguration on **October 14, 1943**. Avoid the unqualified statement that it "never took effect." A safer exam formulation: **Japan exercised de facto control through occupation, while the legitimacy/sovereignty question remained contested and the government-in-exile continued the rival claim.**

## Government in exile and sovereignty

- **Quezon's instructions:** officials left behind (Laurel, Vargas, and others) should **collaborate, but not take an oath of allegiance** to Japan. This kept Filipinos in office instead of letting Japan take over all of government.
- **MacArthur's advice:** take an oath to anything **except the Japanese flag**. Collaborate, but don't change allegiance. This later saved them from being charged and executed after the war.
- **Pomeroy, "New Masters, But the Same Servants":** the same officials served the new occupier.
- **Key point:** Japan had **de facto** control, but the **government in exile (Quezon)** still gave orders, so the **Philippines never lost its control or sovereignty**.

**Link to Foundational concepts:** Japan was a **de facto government by paramount force** (an invading force overwhelming the defenders), not a de jure government.
