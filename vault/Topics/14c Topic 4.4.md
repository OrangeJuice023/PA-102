# 4.4 The Fiscal, Financial, and Monetary Bureaucracy

## Overview and lecture framework

The November 7 lecture notes frame the fiscal, financial, and monetary [[Bureaucracy (Weber)|bureaucracies]] as highly regulated because decisions in these areas carry significant public risks. Fiscal administration concerns the accumulation, utilization, and auditing of government resources. Financial and monetary administration concerns the flow of money and the stability of the financial system.

The lecture's most useful organizing framework is:

1. **Accumulation:** where government resources come from.
2. **Utilization:** how public resources are allocated and spent.
3. **Auditing:** whether public resources were used legally and properly.

## A. Fiscal bureaucracy

### 1. Accumulation: revenue generation

The lecture groups the main revenue-collection institutions as follows:

- **Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR):** assesses and collects internal revenue taxes, fees, and charges; accounts for collections; and helps prevent tax evasion.
- **Bureau of Customs (BOC):** collects customs duties, taxes, fees, and penalties; accounts for customs revenue; and enforces customs laws, including anti-smuggling measures.
- **Tariff Commission:** provides technical work on tariff and trade measures, supporting government revenue and trade-policy decisions.
- **Local Government Units (LGUs):** impose local taxes and fees under the Local Government Code, subject to constitutional and statutory limits. The lecture states that LGUs cannot impose taxes already imposed by the BIR, BOC, or the Tariff Commission, calling the overlap double taxation. This is not a blanket ban on every overlapping tax: it refers to the taxes the Code reserves to the national government under its common limitations (RA 7160, Section 133). Incidental overlap is not automatically illegal double taxation. Local taxation is covered further in [[14f Topic 4.7|Topic 4.7]].

> [!warning] Check
> The notes state the LGU tax bar generally without enumerating Section 133. Confirm the specific reserved taxes against RA 7160 before listing them.

The DOF provides the overall fiscal-policy framework, while these agencies perform important revenue and enforcement functions.

**DOF — Department of Finance**  
**Legal basis:** Administrative Code of 1987, Book IV, Title II.

The DOF is responsible for the sound and efficient management of government financial resources. It formulates fiscal and tax policies and coordinates agencies involved in revenue generation and financial management. The Galindez notes specify the Office of the Secretary, five (5) Undersecretaries and five (5) Assistant Secretaries appointed by the President, the Economic Intelligence and Investigation Bureau, the Services, constituent units, and Regional Offices. Under EO 292, Book IV, Title II, Chapter 2, Section 6, the five Undersecretaries respectively head one Policy Development and Management Services Group — with planning and policy research, central management information, central administration, central financial management, public information and assistance, legal, and regional coordination offices — and four Operations Groups. Each Undersecretary does not head a separate Policy Development and Management Services Group of their own.

**BIR — Bureau of Internal Revenue**  
**Legal basis:** Administrative Code, Book IV, Title II, Chapter 4, Sections 18–22; National Internal Revenue Code, as amended.

The BIR assesses and collects internal revenue taxes, fees, and charges; accounts for revenue collected; exercises lawfully delegated police powers; and works to prevent and prosecute tax evasion and illegal economic activity. It consists of the Commissioner of Internal Revenue and two Deputy Commissioners — one running the Assessment and Collection Group, the other the Legal and Internal Administration Group — all appointed by the President upon the Secretary's recommendation, each with management and technical staff.

**BOC — Bureau of Customs**  
**Legal basis:** Administrative Code, Book IV, Title II, Chapter 4, Sections 23–28; RA 10863, or the Customs Modernization and Tariff Act.

The BOC collects customs duties, taxes, fees, charges, and penalties; accounts for customs revenue; exercises customs police authority; and prevents and suppresses smuggling and customs fraud. Its functions cover imports, exports, ports of entry, vessels, aircraft, and foreign mail. It is comprised of three groups: (1) the Customs Revenue Collection Monitoring Group under a deputy commissioner, with legal, financial, and collection services; (2) the Customs Assessment and Operations Coordinating Group under a deputy commissioner, with imports/assessment and ports operations services; and (3) the Intelligence and Enforcement Group under a deputy commissioner, with intelligence/investigation and enforcement security services.

> [!warning] Check
> The DOF, BIR, and BOC structures above follow the lecture notes' Administrative Code arrangement — confirm current organization against each agency's present charter and orders.

**Tariff Commission**  
**Legal basis:** RA 10863 and related tariff and trade laws.

The Commission provides technical studies and recommendations on tariff matters and trade policy. It supports the protection of government revenue while considering the facilitation of legitimate international trade.

### 2. Utilization: budgeting and expenditure

**Department of Budget and Management (DBM)**  
**Legal basis:** Administrative Code, Book IV, Title XVII, and Book VI.

The DBM prepares, executes, and controls the National Budget. Its functions include:

- Preparing the national resources and expenditures budget.
- Reviewing agency budget proposals and evaluating spending requirements.
- Managing budget execution and controls.
- Maintaining accounting and management systems needed for government operations.
- Administering compensation and position-classification systems.
- Assessing organizational effectiveness.
- Reviewing legislative proposals with budgetary or organizational implications.

The lecture emphasizes that the national budget is an instrument of national development. Budget decisions therefore reflect policy priorities, not just accounting.

### 3. Institutions connecting accumulation and utilization

The Second Year notes label DOF, BTr, and DepDev the **"in-betweens"**: each does both accumulation and utilization — DOF through the BESF and public debt, BTr through the Treasury Single Account, and DepDev through the Philippine Development Plan. They connect the two sides because revenue must be held, managed against debt, matched to planned priorities, and released for spending: generation alone is not a budget, and allocation alone is not cash.

**Bureau of the Treasury (BTr)**  
**Legal basis:** Administrative Code, Book IV, Title II, Chapter 4, Sections 29–32.

Under Sections 29–32: the BTr performs Treasury functions (Sec. 29) — managing government cash resources, public debt, and government securities, connected in the November 7 discussion to the **Treasury Single Account (TSA)** and special, trust, and fiduciary fund balances. It is composed (Sec. 30) of the National Treasurer with two sub-sectors — Internal Affairs and National Government Affairs — each under an Assistant National Treasurer. The National Treasurer is assisted (Sec. 31) by management and technical staff and an intel and investigation office, and the two Assistant National Treasurers are appointed by the President upon the Secretary's recommendation (Sec. 32).

> [!warning] Check
> This BTr structure follows the lecture notes' Administrative Code arrangement — confirm current organization against the Bureau's present orders.

**Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DepDev)**  
**Legal basis:** Article XII, Section 9 of the Constitution; RA 12145 (2025).

RA 12145 reorganized NEDA into DepDev and reconstituted the NEDA Board as the Economy and Development Council. DepDev is the government's primary agency for national and regional economic planning, policy formulation, coordination, and monitoring. Its plans inform public-investment priorities and budget discussions.

### Budget documents discussed in class

| Instrument | Basic function |
|---|---|
| **BESF** — Budget of Expenditures and Sources of Financing | Presents the proposed expenditure program and the sources of financing. It connects the resource side with the spending side of the budget. |
| **NEP** — National Expenditure Program | The Executive's proposed national budget submitted to Congress. |
| **GAB** — General Appropriations Bill | The proposed appropriations measure considered by Congress. |
| **GAA** — General Appropriations Act | The enacted appropriations law authorizing government spending for the fiscal year. |
| **TSA** — Treasury Single Account | A unified structure for government cash resources and accounts, subject to applicable legal and administrative arrangements. |

The class notes use an illustrative classroom example — not a claim about actual UP appropriations — involving a change in the allocation for UP between the NEP, GAB, and GAA. The point is that congressional deliberations may change individual allocations before enactment, subject to constitutional and statutory budget rules, including the Article VI, Section 25(1) bar on increasing the President's recommended operating appropriations.

The Second Year notes state that "Unprogrammed Appropriations are illegal" and that every item must be itemized. Preserve this as the lecture-note statement; do not repeat it as an absolute legal rule.

> [!warning] Check
> Unprogrammed Appropriations are a distinct budget category subject to legal conditions and current judicial directives — not every unprogrammed item is illegal. On October 20, 2026, the Supreme Court invalidated specific increases or provisions involving the 2024, 2025, and 2026 budgets (not a blanket declaration): https://sc.judiciary.gov.ph/sc-holds-unprogrammed-appropriations-subject-to-guidelines-voids-congressional-increases-in-2024-and-2025-budgets/ The DBM's October 25, 2026 statement says unaffected provisions remain implementable subject to their legal bases, funding conditions, and budget procedures: https://www.dbm.gov.ph/index.php/management-2/4332-dbm-to-comply-with-sc-directives-on-2026-ua-unaffected-budget-provisions-remain-implementable Verify current status before quoting.

> [!warning] Check
> The notes describe the NEP, GAB, and GAA as having the same total amount and say that allocations can be moved around. Do not turn this shorthand into a blanket legal rule without checking the Constitution, budget circulars, and the particular GAA. Similarly, the notes call the TSA a “Treasury savings account”; the correct term is **Treasury Single Account**.

### 4. Auditing: accountability for public expenditure

**Commission on Audit (COA)**  
**Legal basis:** 1987 Constitution, Article IX-D, particularly Section 2(2); Presidential Decree No. 1445, Government Auditing Code of the Philippines.

COA examines, audits, and settles government accounts involving revenue, receipts, expenditures, and public property. It has authority to define the scope of audit, establish audit techniques and methods, and promulgate accounting and auditing rules. These rules include the prevention and disallowance of irregular, unnecessary, excessive, extravagant, or unconscionable expenditures or uses of government funds and property.

> [!plain] Source note
> Citation and title confirmed: Presidential Decree No. 1445, Government Auditing Code of the Philippines. Reference: https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/26/59533

The November 7 lecture distinguishes COA's external audit from internal auditing performed by individual agencies. Internal audit does not displace COA's constitutional authority. See also the COA composition and mandate in [[14b Topic 4.3|Topic 4.3]].

## B. Financial and monetary bureaucracy

The financial and monetary bureaucracy deals with the flow of money in the economy, the stability of banks and financial institutions, and the regulation of financial markets.

### 1. Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas and Monetary Board

**BSP**  
**Legal basis:** 1987 Constitution, Article XII, Section 20; RA 7653, or the New Central Bank Act, as amended by RA 11211.

The BSP is the independent central monetary authority. Its core functions include monetary policy, price stability, currency issuance, and supervision and regulation of banks and other financial institutions. RA 11211 strengthened the BSP's capitalization and expanded statutory references to financial stability and payment-system oversight.

The **Monetary Board** is the BSP's principal policy-making body. The lecture identifies the BSP as the central institution for monetary policy and prudential regulation.

The Second Year notes discuss the Philippines adopting Basel standards (rendered "Basel Conventions" in the notes): "we have all the Basel [standards] but we're not a member; we implemented them."

> [!warning] Check
> Do not present "all" as verified, and confirm the membership-and-implementation claim against current BSP sources before repeating it.

### 2. Prudential governance and PDIC

**Philippine Deposit Insurance Corporation (PDIC)**  
**Legal basis:** RA 3591, as amended by RA 9576, RA 10846, and RA 11840.

PDIC protects depositors through deposit insurance and supports financial stability. Its functions include deposit insurance, bank examination in coordination with the BSP, and the receivership and liquidation of closed banks. The lecture traces the liquidation role: bank liquidation used to belong to the BSP and passed to PDIC only when the BSP could not do it, but since 1992 it has been legally transferred to the PDIC.

> [!plain] Source note
> PDIC became the mandatory receiver and liquidator of closed banks in 1992 under RA 7400. The "used to belong to the BSP" framing above is the lecturer's history; the statute states the mandate.

**Current coverage:** The maximum deposit insurance coverage is **₱1 million per depositor, per bank**, effective March 15, 2025. The older notes mentioning ₱500,000 are outdated. The PDIC's official notice confirms the current amount.

> [!plain] Source note
> The ₱1 million coverage (the Galindez notes say ₱500,000) is an external update beyond the older lecture notes. See the PDIC's maximum deposit insurance coverage page: https://www.pdic.gov.ph/mdic

### 3. Corporate securities and insurance regulation

**Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)**  
**Legal basis:** Revised Corporation Code, RA 11232; Securities Regulation Code, RA 8799.

The SEC regulates the creation and operation of corporations and supervises the securities market. The Revised Corporation Code governs private corporations, including corporate organization and governance. The Securities Regulation Code aims to protect investors and promote fair and efficient capital markets. Under RA 8799 the SEC is headed by a five-member commission — a Chairperson and four Commissioners — with seven-year terms.

> [!plain] Source note
> Both numbers and titles confirmed: RA 11232, Revised Corporation Code (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2019/ra_11232_2019.html), and RA 8799, Securities Regulation Code (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2000/ra_8799_2000.html).

**Insurance Commission**  
**Legal basis:** Insurance Code, as amended; Pre-Need Code.

The Insurance Commission supervises insurance and pre-need providers. The notes highlight stronger capitalization requirements, microinsurance regulation, modernization of insurance rules, and enhanced regulatory powers. The lecture discussions also cover bancassurance and the removal of spousal-consent barriers. Bancassurance allows an insurance company to present and sell its products within the premises of a BSP-licensed bank. The bank is not required to have equity ownership in the insurance company.

> [!warning] Check
> The notes cite a specific minimum capital figure for new domestic stock insurers; it is not repeated here. Confirm any threshold against the Insurance Code as amended and current Insurance Commission rules, and distinguish these lecture observations from current legal requirements.

The Nov. 7 lecture groups BSP, PDIC, SEC, and the Insurance Commission within the wider financial-stability system. They remain separate regulators with distinct mandates. The SEC is not a unit under the BSP.

## C. Related professional regulators

The notes also identify Professional Regulatory Boards, including Accountancy and Customs Brokers, operating under the Professional Regulation Commission. They regulate professional practice and standards in their respective fields. The lecture notes refer to 46 PRBs under the PRC. This is related to financial administration but should not be confused with the core fiscal and monetary agencies.

> [!plain] Source note
> The PRC confirms 46 Professional Regulatory Boards. Source: https://prc.gov.ph/professional-regulatory-boards

## Key takeaways for 4.4

- Fiscal administration can be organized into accumulation, utilization, and audit.
- BIR, BOC, Tariff Commission, and LGUs are relevant to revenue generation.
- DBM leads national budgeting and expenditure management.
- DOF, BTr, and DepDev connect resource generation, cash/debt management, and development planning.
- COA is the constitutional external auditor.
- BSP/Monetary Board, PDIC, SEC, and the Insurance Commission contribute to financial stability through separate mandates.
- Budget figures and terminology in class shorthand should be checked against formal budget documents before being presented as legal rules.
- Lecture shorthand on moved allocations, "illegal" unprogrammed appropriations, and the LGU tax bar needs confirmation against the GAA, current directives, and RA 7160.
