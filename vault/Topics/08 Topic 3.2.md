# Topic 3.2: Spanish colonization (1521; 1565 to 1898)

> [!plain] In plain words
> Spain built a [[Decentralization|centralized]] colonial state that mixed church, military, and government power. Filipinos first asked for reforms inside Spain, then fought for independence, and built their own government along the way. The Revolution was also state-building.

## Conquest and the colonial state

- **Sir's conquest structure:** **Friar → Captain → Army** (religious + political + military power).
- **1521** = first contact (**Magellan**). **1565** = permanent rule begins (**Legazpi**).
- **Precolonial political communities → Spanish conquest → colonial state.**
- **Elizalde:** Spanish rule (1565–1898) changed over time: different colonial models and economic regimes reorganized political, economic, social, and cultural life.

**Colonial hierarchy:** King of Spain → Consejo de Indias / Ministerio de Ultramar → Viceroyalty of Nueva España (until 1821) → Governor-General → Provinces → Pueblos/Municipalities → Barrios.

| Level | Office | Functions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central | **Governor-General** (Gobernador y Capitán General) | Representative of the King, commander-in-chief, chief justice, vice-royal patron. One office, many powers. |
| Provincial | **Alcaldía**: **Alcalde Mayor** | Broad executive, judicial, police, tribute, inspection, patronage, military functions. Could trade under the **indulto de comercio** (Agoncillo). |
| Provincial | **Corregimiento**: **Corregidor** |  |
| Pueblo | **Gobernadorcillo** | Head of the municipality. Filipinos and Chinese mestizos could serve; needed age, Spanish literacy, and prior service as cabeza de barangay. Aides: directorcillo, alguaciles, tenientes de justicia. |
| Barrio | **Cabeza de Barangay** | Tax/tribute collection, peace and order, recruiting **polistas**, local admin. Tenientes del barrio. |

## Accountability: Residencia and Visita

|  | Residencia | Visita |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When | **After** the official's term | **During** the term |
| By whom | Investigation of the official's conduct | A **Visitador-General** |

Possible penalties: fines, dismissal, imprisonment, property sequestration.

## Church, economy, and society

- **Church and State** were deeply intertwined. The clergy influenced civil administration, education, censorship, land, economic affairs, local politics. **Friarocracy / monastic supremacy** became a major 19th-century criticism → **separation of Church and State** in [[Malolos Constitution|Malolos]].
- **Economic institutions:** taxation/tribute; **polo y servicio personal** (forced community labor); **encomienda** (grants of tribute rights over populations); **Galleon Trade** (Manila–Acapulco); Real Sociedad Económica de Amigos del País; Royal Philippine Company.
- **República de Españoles vs. República de Indios:** separate legal orders under the Recopilación de Leyes de los Reinos de las Indias.
- Spanish rule reorganized society through admin categories, taxes, labor duties, religion, education, economic rules, changing local elites.
- **Habsburg → Bourbon:** more **centralist, interventionist, reform-oriented**.

## Cádiz Constitution of 1812

- **Core ideas:** national [[Sovereignty|sovereignty]], constitutional monarchy, [[Separation of Powers|separation of powers]], representation, individual liberties, political participation.
- The PH was recognized as part of the Spanish nation with **representation in the Cortes**. Initial goal: **reform + representation + assimilation**.
- Later: stronger Spanish intervention in local government and **removal of PH representation**.
- **Aguilar (2025)** studies this tension.
- **High-yield line:** **constitutional equality on paper ↔ colonial hierarchy in practice.**

## From reform to revolution

**La Propaganda:** sought change **within** the Spanish system: equality of Filipinos and Spaniards, PH representation, restored Filipino role in local government, reforms in Church and government.

|  | La Liga Filipina | Katipunan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founded | **1892**, by **José Rizal** | **1892** |
| Goal | **Reform**: unity, mutual protection, defense against injustice, education, agriculture, commerce, social reform | **Independence** + revolutionary action |
| Nature | Reformist, **not armed** | **Political and moral**, not only military |
| Structure | **Supreme Council → Provincial Councils → Popular Councils** | Central council + local units |
| Fate | Weakened after Rizal's arrest and deportation | Led the Revolution |

**Kartilya ng Katipunan:** equality, brotherhood, defense of the oppressed, good character, duty, respect for women, equality regardless of status or race. **Formula: personal morality → social equality → national liberation.**

**Rizal and the Noli (Schumacher):** the Noli was a **catalyst**, not the direct cause, of the Revolution. Don't reduce it to "Noli → Revolution." It helped people understand colonial society, build national consciousness, question inequality, and think about Filipino identity. **Rizal's path: reform → disillusionment → national consciousness → eventual independence.**

## Revolutionary governments

- **Tejeros Convention (March 22, 1897):** **Magdiwang vs. Magdalo** (Cavite factions). Question: should the Katipunan stay the revolutionary government, or be replaced? A central revolutionary government was set up: **Aguinaldo President**, **Mariano Trías VP**. The **Bonifacio-Tirona** conflict created a **legitimacy crisis**. Katipunan as authority → formal revolutionary government → legitimacy problem.
- **Departmental Government of Central Luzon:** President, VP, Treasury, Interior, War, Welfare. Revolutionaries were already experimenting with **departmental government**, not just military command.
- **Biak-na-Bato Republic:** President, VP, and departments (Treasury, Interior, War, Foreign Affairs). Its provisional constitution recognized religious liberty, education, press freedom, freedom of professions. Moved toward an independent state while keeping some reformist demands.
- **Hong Kong Junta:** center for diplomacy, financing, planning, procurement, political decisions during exile.
- **Aguinaldo's governments:** **[[Pangulo Regime|Pangulo]] → Dictator → Revolutionary Government → Constitutional Republic.**

## Malolos Republic

- Brought together executive departments, legislature, local government, judiciary, and constitutional rules.
- **Malolos Constitution:** free and independent republic, popular sovereignty, representative and responsible government, 3 branches, religious freedom, **separation of Church and State**.
- **Structure:** Republic → Legislature (Assembly), Executive (President + Cabinet), Judiciary (Supreme Court). Features: separation of powers, continuing legislature, strong President of the Cabinet, accountability to the legislature, indirect elections.
- **Sir's final slide:** a shift toward **Haring Bayang Katagalugan**, later **Filipinas**; separation of Church and State; national unity; international recognition; government should **not stay in military hands**.

## Filipino political thought

| La Liga / Propaganda | Katipunan and after |
| --- | --- |
| Representation + equality + reform | Independence + self-determination + national unity |

Resistance **before** La Liga was often local, economic, parochial, and defensive of native ways.

## Most important idea of 3.2

**The Revolution was state-building:** secret society → revolutionary movement → provisional governments → departments → congress → constitution → republic.

**Whole lecture in one chain:** colonial state → liberal ideas / Cádiz → reform movement → La Liga → Katipunan → revolutionary governments → Malolos Republic.
