# Topic 4.1: Hyper Pangulo

> [!verify] \[NEEDS VERIFICATION\]

> [!plain] In plain words
> there isn't one [[Bureaucracy (Weber)|bureaucracy]] but many, and all of them answer to the President. The PH has a **[[Pangulo Regime|pangulo regime]]** where the **executive is supreme**, rooted in the value of **[[Pagdamay|pagdamay]]** (fraternity). It's "hyper" because many gaps let the President dominate.

**Sources:** your 4.1 notes, cross-checked with Galindez. The President's specific powers (control, qualified political agency, reorganization, appointments) are in **OCT 3 - RAW NOTES**, so they aren't repeated here.

## Not one bureaucracy, but many

- Traditionally it was all just "the government." Intellectual progress split it into the **political** side (political positions) and the **technical/administrative** side (the bureaucracy).
- A **functional view** shows bureaucracies **inside every part** of government: each branch has its own, and so do overlooked areas.

| Bureaucracy | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Each branch** | Its own bureaucracy |
| **Independent bodies** | Their own bureaucracies |
| **State finance** | **Fiscal** = public funds and allocation. **Monetary** = banks and interest rates; policies that push you to save or spend; shapes the economy. |
| **Public corporations** | **LGUs** are public corporations too. **GOCCs** (government enterprises), e.g., Pag-IBIG, LandBank, each with different structures and rules. |
| **National security** | Defense + internal order/law enforcement. The **National Security Council** sits directly below the President as an intermediary body. The **military** bureaucracy is separate; the armed forces don't go to department secretaries for clearance. |
| **Law enforcement** | **General:** PNP. **Specialized:** SAF, Coast Guard |
| **Regional** | **[[Autonomy (CAR vs BARMM)\|Autonomous]] regions** (Bangsamoro) and **administrative regions** (because of Mindanao and the Cordillera) |
| **Intergovernmental** | Bureaucracies that link levels of government |

**All these bureaucracies are run by the President.** That's the Philippine pangulo regime.

## Agpalo's pangulo regime

| Regime | Origin | Supremacy | Core value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Parliamentary** (Westminster) | UK | **Legislature** ("parliamentary [[Sovereignty\|sovereignty]]") | **Liberty** (democracy) |
| **Presidential** | US | **Balance**: [[Separation of Powers\|separation of powers]], executive equal to legislative | **Equality** |
| **Pangulo** | PH | **Executive** | **Fraternity / pagdamay** |

**Why pagdamay?** Under Spain we lacked **liberty**; under America we faced **inequality**. We fought for freedom, then independence and equality, yet our regime centered on **fraternity**. Pagdamay runs through **La Propaganda** (people voluntarily funded **La Solidaridad**), **La Liga**, and **Rizal**. It was a **core value of the First Philippine Republic**.

**The [[Malolos Constitution|Malolos]] puzzle:** if Malolos emphasized legislative supremacy, how did the First Republic become a pangulo regime?

1. **On paper, legislative supremacy:** the Malolos Constitution set three powers and put legislative power in the **Assembly of Representatives (Art. 33)**. The **President was elected by the Assembly**, not the people. Designed by **Felipe Calderón** as parliamentary.
2. **In practice (Agpalo):** the **transitory provisions** changed how it worked. **Art. 99** let the government, while Congress was closed during the independence war, **resolve unforeseen questions by decree**.
3. So **Agpalo calls Aguinaldo's First Republic a "true and real pangulo regime."** After the Treaty of Paris and the Philippine-American War, Aguinaldo as "dictator" held legislative power as a **transitory** measure, since no legislature could be appointed yet. He went from dictator to president.

**Presidents and legislative power:**

| President | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| **Aguinaldo** | Dictator, then President; took legislative power as **transitory** |
| **Marcos** | **Usurped** legislative power entirely |
| **Corazon Aquino** | 1986 Freedom Constitution; laws issued as **executive orders**, e.g., the **Administrative Code (EO 292)** and the **Family Code**. Like Aguinaldo's, her decrees were **transitory**. |
| **Gloria Arroyo** | Declared a **state of national emergency**, using [[Emergency Powers\|emergency powers]]. Questioned in court. **SC:** the President may declare it, but **only Congress can grant the exercise of emergency powers**. Seen as an attempt to create a regime. |

> Galindez wrote that the SC said Arroyo "can declare [[Martial Law|martial law]]." Your 4.1 notes say **state of national emergency**. Go with your notes, but confirm.

**Regime vs. model of a regime:**

- A **regime** is **institutionalized**: a **constitution**, an **ongoing government**, **national symbols**, **armed forces**, **national anthem**, and other paraphernalia (e.g., a national university).
- Later presidents didn't build regimes; they **copied Quezon's model**. Philippine presidents give us a **model** of a pangulo regime.
- The President's many roles are listed on pp. 53–54 of the reading.

## The Office of the President

- **Symbol:** Malacañang Palace.
- **Structure:** Office of the President → (a) **OP Proper**: Private Office, Executive Office, Common Staff Support System, Presidential Special Assistants/Advisers System; and (b) **Agencies under the OP**.
- **Sec. 31(1):** the President can restructure the **OP Proper**: abolish, consolidate, or merge units, and transfer functions between units.
- **Sec. 31(2):** the President can transfer functions between the **OP and other departments/agencies**.

**Administrative relations (more power → less power):**

| Relation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Supervision and control** | "I can direct and reverse." |
| **Administrative supervision** | "I oversee, but don't micromanage." |
| **Attachment** | "We coordinate laterally." |

## The Vice President

- **Can the VP be appointed to another office?** Yes. The PH VP can be appointed to any office or department and do real work.
- In the US, the VP is a ceremonial **"spare tire"** and the butt of jokes. The PH has used the VP's office to its full potential.
- The VP is a **fiscalizing** office, an **institutional evolution**, and can be a force for good or bad.
- Even without set functions, the VP can be very useful. What the VP does depends on the **President's discretion**.

## Residual powers and assuming office

- **Marcos v. Manglapus (1989):** Marcos, in Hawaii for 3 years, petitioned (mandamus) to return. **Denied for national security**: he'd be a hero to loyalists who wouldn't let go of power, his return would cause unrest between loyalists and People Power supporters, and there were plans to stage a coup against Cory.
- **Residual powers:** the President holds powers from the **entire democratic mandate**, even if not listed in the Constitution. These were part of why Marcos was barred.
- **Assuming office:** the SC said **Arroyo** assumed office through the **direct exercise of democracy**, recognized by the sovereign Filipino people. **Cory** assumed office through **revolution**.
- The President is like an **edge case**: the only real bound is the **Bill of Rights**.

## Immunity from suit

- You **can't sue the President during incumbency**. Origin: the old idea that "the king can do no wrong" (Galindez: a bit contradictory).
- Suits are **deferred**, not erased; they can pile up ("pwedeng maipon").
- Covers **liabilities only**, not accountability.
- **Workaround:** file the case against the **Executive Secretary**.
- **Accountability remedies still available:** **habeas corpus, amparo, habeas data.**

## The President as a symbol above politics

- **Duterte** became a symbol **above politics**, still popular in **May 2025**; he "transcended from a person to a deity" (**political anthropology**).
- Under the **Boy Scouts' organic law**, the President is the **Chief Scout**.
- The President is supposed to be the **head of state**, so the President appointing a justice or members of the Constitutional Commissions is **not** a violation of separation of powers.

## Why "hyper" pangulo

**Many bureaucracies + all under the President + executive supremacy rooted in pagdamay + emergency and residual powers + immunity + symbolic status + appointing power over co-equal bodies = "madaming butas, kaya hyper pangulo."**

The 1987 Constitution combines executive capacity with safeguards. **Pangulo regime = executive supremacy in the Philippine experience.** The lecture's blunt summary: **"Madaming butas kaya hyper pangulo."**
