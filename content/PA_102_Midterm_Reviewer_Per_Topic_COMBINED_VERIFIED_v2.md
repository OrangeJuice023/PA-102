## FOUNDATIONAL CONCEPTS

### Politics–Administration Dichotomy

- Old idea: **politics decides; administration carries it out.**
- Why it emerged:
  - **patronage / spoils system**
  - **Roaring 20s:** wealth increased; belief that government should stay out of the private sector
- Then **Great Depression** → people saw that government has to intervene when the economy fails.
- **Dwight Waldo:** PA should not be only the **3 Es**:
  - efficiency
  - economy
  - effectiveness
  - also needs a **pyramid/pinnacle of values**
- Lecture example: PA can become “cold-hearted” when it only asks **how** to enforce policy and not **why** the problem exists or what values should guide the response.

### State

**1987 Constitution, Art. II, Sec. 1:** Philippines = **democratic and republican state**; sovereignty resides in the people.

Four elements of the State:
1. **People**
2. **Territory**
3. **Government**
4. **Sovereignty**

**Poindexter v. Greenhow:** State as an **“ideal person”** → intangible; acts through government.

**Sovereignty:** freedom from external influence; can be transferred/transmitted to a successor.

### Democratic vs Republican

**Democratic**
- direct exercise of democracy through representatives
- representative can be understood as **conduit, agent, trustee**
- mass participation can also create bandwagoning / mob mentality

**Republican**
- concern with **survival**
- strong military + strong economy
- concern with invasion / outsiders

Together, the democratic + republican character creates the need to define who belongs to the political community.

### Government

Government = **ensemble of political institutions** that impose a mandate and rule people in a territory.

- **Ensemble** = the whole / combination working together
- **Political institutions** = agency + normativity
- **Mandate** = make, enforce, adjudicate
- **Adjudication**
  - **inquisitorial:** judge has a direct investigative role
  - **adversarial:** neutral judge; parties argue against each other

### De Jure vs De Facto Government

**De jure**
- legitimate / duly constituted
- sovereignty over the people

**De facto**
- “of the fact” → occupies / actually rules
- examples in lecture: Japanese occupation

Three senses of de facto government:
1. legal sense: possession of territory + control of formal structure
2. **paramount force:** invading force overwhelms defenders
3. **revolt / insurrection:** overwhelming force from inside the country

Martial law was discussed as a response to **paramount force** and **revolt/insurrection**.

### President — Basic Roles from Lecture

- head of state
- head of government
- chief executive
- general supervisor of LGUs
- national service leader
- commander-in-chief
- policy leader

President as **trustee representative**.

### Bureaucracy — Max Weber

Bureaucracy = **rational-legal authority + division of labor / hierarchy**.

Features:
- specialization
- hierarchy
- rational-legal rules
- impersonality
- merit and fitness
- security of tenure

Why bureaucracy:
- public goods
- externalities
- monopolies
- redistribution of wealth
- social regulation
- information asymmetry

**Policy capacity:** does the state have the capacity to achieve its goals?

**State capacity:** does the state have the resources/capacity to do something?

**Polity**
- **Bureaucratic polity:** technocratic elite
- **Proprietary polity:** weak state institutions; resource elites have strong influence over what the state does

# 2. INSTITUTIONAL OVERVIEW OF THE PHILIPPINES

### Ways of Understanding an Organization / Government

1. **Written down**
   - Constitution
   - statutes
   - administrative issuances
   - jurisprudence / case law
2. **Believed to exist**
   - people's perceptions of government
   - may differ from what is written
3. **Actually exists**
   - org chart
   - citizen's charter
   - mission / vision
   - actual hierarchy and relationships
   - may require **organizational diagnosis** first
4. **Actually needed**
   - identify the issue → diagnose → determine what structure/government is actually needed

### Casis — *If I Were to Reform Philippine Society*

- Reform should be based on **Filipino-owned values**.
- Precolonial society was a **fused society**:
  - **Lakan** — north
  - **Datu** — middle
  - **Sultan** — south
- One leader could perform executive, judicial, legislative, warrior, economic, religious and administrative roles.
- We later **borrowed separation of powers**.
- Problem: Philippine governance can miss the **means of doing** — the “why” behind action, not only the formal structure.
- Connect to **Waldo:** collective values should guide administration.
- Post-Marcos safeguards discussed: **judicial review** and **Ombudsman**.
- Lecture connects Filipino values such as **utang na loob** and **bayanihan** to the need to control favoritism / corruption.

**Core point:** **values make up part of the reform.**

### Tiglao — *The Decapitalization of the Philippines*

**Filipino First Policy**
- associated with **Pres. Carlos P. Garcia**
- Art. XII, Sec. 10
- now discussed as **economic nationalism**

Filipino ownership requirement discussed in class:
- majority of the board = Filipino
- 60% of company = Filipino

Tiglao's problem:
- companies can appear Filipino-owned on paper while foreign interests retain real influence
- **voting preferred shares** were discussed as one way ownership could be diluted on paper

**Lesson:** formal institutions can be followed in appearance while informal arrangements shape the real outcome.

# 2.2 CONSTITUTIONAL DESIGN AND MANDATES OF / ON PUBLIC POWER

## Constitutional Supremacy and Hierarchy of Laws

The Constitution is the **supreme law** and the boundary of public power.

Sir's four words:
- **supreme**
- **imperious**
- **absolute**
- **unalterable**

Basic hierarchy discussed:
**Constitution → statutes → administrative rules/orders**

A law or provision contrary to the Constitution is **null and void**.

### Cases

**Manila Prince Hotel v. GSIS (1997)**
- Filipino First Policy
- **self-executing**
- self-executing = complete in itself; can be enforced by courts without needing another law
- non-self-executing = needs enabling / supplementary legislation

**Tañada v. Angara (1997)**
- read the Constitution **as a whole**
- some provisions are not self-executing

**Sameer Overseas v. Cabiles (2014)**
- unconstitutional provision = treated as inoperative
- key idea: an unconstitutional law confers no rights, imposes no duties, gives no protection, creates no office, and is treated as if it had not been passed

---

## Separation of Powers + Checks and Balances

### Angara v. Electoral Commission (1936)
- branches are **independent and co-equal**
- separation of powers comes from the constitutional allocation of powers in **Arts. VI, VII, VIII**

### Macalintal v. COMELEC (2003) — Puno, J.
- prevents concentration of legislative, executive, and judicial powers in one branch
- roots traced to **Aristotle**; modern formulation associated with **Locke and Montesquieu**

### Planas v. Gil (1939)
- branches are not divided into **“watertight compartments”**
- there is real **interdependence** between branches

**Main idea:** separation of powers ≠ total isolation. Each branch is supreme in its sphere, but checks and balances create interaction.

## 4 KINDS OF PUBLIC POWER / FUNCTION

## Constituent vs Ministrant

**Constituent functions**
- constitute the **very bonds of society**
- compulsory

Eight functions:
1. keep order / protect persons and property
2. fix legal relations of family
3. regulate holding / transfer / exchange of property and related liabilities
4. determine contract rights
5. define and punish crime
6. administer civil justice
7. determine political duties, privileges, and relations of citizens
8. deal with foreign powers / external danger / international interests

**Ministrant functions**
- advance the general interests of society
- described as optional / support functions that can also be managed by the private sector
- public works
- public education
- public charity
- health and safety regulation
- trade and industry regulation
- lecture notes also mention inventions / scientific discovery

## Governmental vs Proprietary

**Governmental**
- exercise of state powers
- promotes public welfare
- legislative, judicial, public, political powers
- connected to exercise of public office
- state generally has immunity from suit for pure governmental functions, unless waived

**Proprietary**
- special benefit / advantage of the community
- ministerial, private, corporate functions
- government acts as an **economic entity**
- government strips away the sovereign mantle and can be liable like a private entity

# 2.3 INFORMAL INSTITUTIONS

**Formal institution** = what the rules officially say.

**Informal institution** = socially established rule, norm, relationship, or practice affecting behavior even without formal codification.

**Core relationship:**
**formal rules + informal norms → actual behavior → governance**

## Utang na Loob — Cleofas

- **debt of good will** from receiving another person's benevolent assistance
- creates an expectation of reciprocity

Not automatically bad.

Good:
- gratitude
- reciprocity
- helping someone who helped you
- maintaining relationships
- communal solidarity

Problematic when:
**personal obligation → favoritism → others disadvantaged**

Important value: **katarungan (justice/fairness)**.

The goal is for utang na loob to contribute to **pagkakaisa** and communal flourishing, not undermine fair distribution of opportunities/resources.

### Parientes–Padrinos / Patron–Client

**Patron** → resources / access / assistance

**Client** → gratitude / obligation / loyalty

Can happen around:
- kasal
- binyag
- libing
- fiestas

Important PA problem:
**weak public service → personal connection → special access → utang na loob**

Personal relationships may **compensate for weak institutions** while also **reinforcing the weakness**.

## De Joya et al. — Utang na Loob and Voting

- study in **Bacoor and Imus, Cavite**
- 8 registered voters
- semi-structured interviews
- **Jocano's SIR** model + consequentialist approach

**SIR:** keeping harmonious relationships; belongingness, reciprocity, hiya, avoiding conflict.

Political favors can include:
- money
- food
- liquor
- gifts
- help around social events

Chain:
**favor → gratitude → obligation → loyalty**

Main point:
- gratitude itself is not the problem
- problem is when personal obligation overrides fair evaluation of candidates and performance
- study is small and qualitative; cannot be generalized to all Filipino voters

**Additional detail from friend's reviewer:**
- The notes report that lower-income voters were more heavily targeted and that the feeling of debt could be stronger at lower socioeconomic levels.
- Most respondents did not consider utang na loob an ethical basis for voting because government service is part of a politician's job; the notes identify **Class E** as an exception emphasizing immediate help.
- Existing political power, lack of alternatives, and immediate benefits were also discussed as reasons voters may still support favor-giving politicians.

## Diskarte

**Morales (2017)**
- creative problem-solving in response to practical problems / situational constraints
- resourcefulness, flexibility, adapting to conditions
- diskarte is **reactive and adaptive**
- not simply intelligence
- not exactly the same as creativity
- practical does not automatically mean ethical

**Hilotin**
- diskarte can also be used to justify rule-bending, fixers, kickbacks, and “hustle” behavior
- tribalism / loyalty can cause selective blindness:
  - “Magnanakaw siya, pero amin siya.”
  - conspiracy framing
  - regionalism
  - moral outsourcing
  - whataboutism

Lecture solutions discussed:
- transparency
- traceable spending
- consequences / punishment
- reduce structures that allow fixer / dynasty politics
- protect whistleblowers

## Pakikisama — Ibe

**Pakikisama** = getting along; maintaining harmonious relationships.

Positive:
- cooperation
- social cohesion
- community

Negative:
- conformity
- avoiding necessary disagreement
- protecting relationships over accountability

“Extended political family” → personal/family loyalty can feed patronage and dynasties.

## Hiya

**Hiya** = shame / sensitivity to social judgment / concern about relationships.

Positive:
- consideration for others

Negative:
- silence
- reluctance to report wrongdoing

PA example:
**“Report the wrongdoing.”**
vs.
**“Wag na, nakakahiya.”**

Mendoza: **hiya, tino, bait** as “vernacular grammars” people use in interpreting scandals.

Examples:
- lower prices
- jobs / livelihood
- health care
- education
- security
- infrastructure
- accountability
- welfare

Important chain:
**demand → government → law + budget + capacity → policy → implementation**

Citizen asks: **“Ano ang kailangan namin ngayon?”**

Government asks: **“Ano ang kaya naming gawin legally, financially, administratively?”**

### Baroña / Pulse Asia

Main lecture point: citizens have **concrete and immediate demands**, especially around cost of living and livelihood.

Do not focus only on exact percentages; the point is what people are asking government to solve.

### Street-level dilemma

General rule meets complicated individual cases.

Example:
- 100 applicants
- resources for 40
- all have real needs

**general rule → different real people → limited resources → frontline judgment**

Question:
**How do officials apply general rules to messy individual cases?**

Formal pressure:
- “Follow the rule.”

Informal pressure:
- “Ma'am, tulungan niyo naman ako.”
- “Kakilala ko si Mayor.”
- “Utang na loob naman.”

This is where informal institutions enter actual public service delivery.

# 3.1 PRECOLONIAL PERIOD

Main idea:
**no nation-state ≠ no government**

Precolonial Philippines had:
- political communities
- leadership
- customary law
- alliances
- trade
- tribute
- warfare
- social hierarchy
- functional specialization
- maritime connections

### Burling — Hills and Plains

**Hills**
- difficult terrain
- lower population density
- shifting cultivation
- more local political autonomy
- smaller communities

**Plains**
- easier communication
- denser populations
- wet-rice agriculture
- larger / more integrated political communities

The contrast is not absolute. Communities traded, migrated, intermarried, and fought.

### Abinales & Amoroso — Family / Kinship

- **cognatic kinship** = descent through both father's and mother's sides
- **fictive kinship** = non-blood ties producing loyalty / cooperation

Politics relied heavily on kinship, loyalty, and personal relationships.

### Barangay / “Baranganic” Societies

Sir's point: **barangay ≠ just a tiny village**.

**Jocano**
- barangay had specialists, trade networks, social stratification, politico-administrative organization
- for practical purposes, described as a **State**

**Junker**
- more cautious: **pre-state complex society / chiefdom**
- hereditary elites
- regional integration
- temporary alliances
- segmented political structures

Key distinction:
**chiefdom ≠ modern state**

Barangay characteristics discussed:
- territorial rights
- residence-based membership
- government
- customary law
- punishment
- leadership
- personal relationships and changing alliances

### Datu and Other Roles

**Datu**
- political/social leader
- legislation
- judging / arbitration
- mediation
- warfare

Other roles:
- **Atubang sa Datu** — administrative role
- **Council of Elders** — advice / community authority
- **Baylan / Catalonan** — spiritual authority; could be a woman or queer man (lecture)
- **Umalohokan / Paratawag** — announcer
- tribute collector

Important:
**functional specialization** existed.

The datu was not simply an unlimited ruler. Customary law and community expectations could bind leadership.

### Junker — Sources / Political Economy

More political complexity can coexist with fragmentation.

Economy was not only foreign trade:
- agriculture
- local production
- crafts
- internal trade
- tribute
- prestige goods
- warfare
- social obligations

Sources should be read critically:
**source → author + context + purpose + worldview → interpretation**

### Talib — Sea as Political Space

Sea was not just a barrier.

**geo-political + geo-strategic + geo-economic**

**tanah air** / “land-water” / liquid world

**sea → movement → trade → interaction → political/economic power**

### Maritime Asia

PH was part of **Maritime Asia**, not isolated.

Possible penalties:

- **encomienda**

# 3.3 AMERICAN COLONIZATION

Main idea:
US rule built institutions and Filipino participation while **retaining US sovereignty**.

### Legal / Political Status

- PH became an **unincorporated US territory**
- First Philippine Republic was treated in the lecture notes as a **belligerent de facto** government
- American administration replaced existing political authority

### McKinley / State-Building

Orders discussed:
1. establish order + municipal government
2. build the state + conquer remaining territory
3. govern for the “happiness” of Filipinos, even while conforming to existing prejudices, including Christian/non-Christian distinctions

### Three priorities of American state-building

**1. Bureau of Civil Service**
- early major institution
- selection based on character and fitness
- goal: bureaucracy as a **regime of law, not of men**
- reduce partisan interference

**2. Public education**
- free schooling
- English as common language in the American system
- education tied to preparing citizens / government participation

**3. Philippine Constabulary (1901)**
- gendarmerie / military-style police
- firearms regulation
- law enforcement
- health emergency functions
- telegraph / postal support
- suppression of brigandage and insurrection
- supervised local police

Lecture connection:
**militarized governance** — police/military institutions took on broad state functions.

### Land / Tax Failures

- land policy did not successfully break tenancy
- tax policy burden was uneven between poorer peasants and wealthier landowners

### Special Areas: Mindanao / Cordillera

- weak Spanish control in many areas
- American government created special arrangements under military control
- **Moro Province** as a distinct administrative arrangement
- Bureau of Non-Christian Tribes used ethno-racial categories
- non-Christian peoples were treated as outside the dominant definition of “Filipino” in the colonial framework

Legacy discussed:
- non-Christian groups became minority outsiders
- American mapping influenced later Bangsamoro territorial questions

### Organic Laws

**[VERIFIED FACT-CHECK] Civil Service Act:** **Act No. 5 was enacted September 19, 1900.** Do not memorize any “November 31, 1900” date; November has only 30 days.

**[VERIFIED FACT-CHECK] Rafael Crame:** Rafael Crame became the **first Filipino Chief of the Philippine Constabulary in 1917**. He was not its first chief overall.

**[VERIFIED FACT-CHECK] Commonwealth:** The Commonwealth was inaugurated on **November 15, 1935**. The 1936 date in some notes refers to Constitution Day, not the Commonwealth inauguration.

**Philippine Bill / Cooper Act (1902)**
- basic colonial organic law
- bill of rights
- transitional constitutional framework

**Jones Law (1916)**
- Philippine Commission → Senate
- Senate President introduced the local use of that title

**Commonwealth (1935)**
- own national government framework
- President + VP + Cabinet

# 3.5 THE BIRTH OF THE MESTIZO — AGABIN

### Main idea

The Philippine legal system became **hybrid**.

**Spanish Civil Law + American Common Law / constitutional-public law + local/customary law = mestizo legal system**

“Mestizo” is a **legal hybridization metaphor**, not a racial description.

Americanization was strongest in:
- public law
- constitutional law
- procedure
- jurisprudence
- commercial law

Spanish foundation remained strong in:
- private civil law
- family
- property
- contracts
- succession
- much criminal law

### Conquest → Penetration

Three levels:
1. **political** — occupation / annexation / colonial government
2. **institutional** — courts, legislation, administration, procedure
3. **jurisprudential** — doctrine, precedent, due process, police power, common-law reasoning

### American Expansion / Annexation Context

- Feb. 15, 1898 — USS Maine explosion
- May 1, 1898 — Battle of Manila Bay; George Dewey defeats Spanish fleet
- Dec. 10, 1898 — Treaty of Paris; Spain cedes PH to US for $20 million
- Feb. 1899 — Philippine-American War
- US War Department, under Elihu Root, handled Philippine affairs

### Policy of Attraction

William Howard Taft cultivated Filipino **ilustrados / elites** as colonial intermediaries.

**Partido Federalista** = elite accommodation, legal/property security, closer US integration.

### Insular Cases / Constitutionalism

The US Constitution did not simply “follow the flag” in full.

The PH was treated as an **unincorporated territory**.

Colonial contradiction:
**constitutional government was taught while colonial sovereignty remained American.**

### Police Power

State power to regulate private conduct/property/business for public welfare and self-protection.

Three ideas from lecture:
- self-protection
- state authority
- social function

Private rights are not automatically absolute when public welfare is involved.

### Cases — Police Power / Due Process

**United States v. Ling Su Fan (1908)**
- regulation upheld under police power

**United States v. Toribio (1910)**
- carabao slaughter restriction upheld in agricultural emergency

**De Villata v. Stanley (1915)**
- public-utility licensing condition involving government mail carriage
- initially upheld; later reversed by US Supreme Court
- tension: **police power vs due process/property rights**

### Procedural vs Substantive Due Process

**Procedural** = was proper procedure followed?

**Substantive** = is the government action itself constitutionally permissible?

Lecture progression:
**police power emphasis → 1920s substantive due process → stronger protection of property / economic liberty / freedom of contract**

### Important 1920s Cases

**United States v. Ang Tang Ho (1922)**
- rice price-fixing executive order invalidated
- economic liberty / property

**People v. Pomar (1924)**
- maternity benefit law struck down
- liberty of contract

**Yu Cong Eng v. Trinidad (1926)**
- Chinese bookkeeping law invalidated
- substantive due process

### Procedure: Inquisitorial → Accusatorial / Adversarial

**Spanish:** inquisitorial
- stronger investigative court role

**American:** accusatorial / adversarial
- prosecution and defense as opposing parties
- neutral judge

**General Order No. 68**
- civil marriage / reduced exclusive ecclesiastical jurisdiction

**General Order No. 58 (1900)**
- American-style accusatorial criminal procedure
- stronger protection for accused
- no jury trial

**Code of Civil Procedure (1901)**
- public trials
- American-style litigation

### Exclusionary Rule — Case Progression

**Spanish / early period:** illegally obtained evidence generally admissible

**Adams v. New York tradition:** early American period also admitted such evidence

**Alvarez v. CFI of Tayabas (1937)**
- movement toward exclusion / return of evidence under certain conditions

**Moncado v. People’s Court (1948)**
- returned to admission

**Stonehill v. Diokno (1967)**
- overruled Moncado
- exclusionary rule firmly established

Memory:
**ADMISSIBLE → LIMITED EXCLUSION → ADMISSIBLE AGAIN → STONEHILL / EXCLUSION**

### Private Law / Criminal Law

**Civil Code of 1889** remained important in:
- family
- property
- contracts
- succession
- personal status

**Spanish Penal Code of 1870** remained foundational → basis of Revised Penal Code.

American additions included:
- Treason / Insurrection / Sedition laws
- Libel Law
- Perjury Law

**People v. Perez (1923)**
- dangerous tendency test in political speech context

### Common-law Hybridization

**Torts / quasi-delicts**
- proximate cause
- contributory negligence
- last clear chance
- vicarious liability
- sovereign immunity
- **Picart v. Smith** → reasonable person / reasonable man standard

**Contracts**
- **causa** + **consideration**
- promissory estoppel
- laches
- estoppel

**Succession**
- heir / devisee / legatee distinctions

**Fianza**
- surety = more directly bound
- guaranty = collateral / conditional

**Property**
- Spanish movables / immovables remain
- Anglo-American land/personalty, equity, trusts enter

### Commercial Law — Strongest Americanization

- Corporation Law — **Act No. 1459**
- Negotiable Instruments Law — **Act No. 2031**
- Insolvency / Bankruptcy Act — **Act No. 1956**
- Warehouse Receipts Law — **Act No. 2137**
- Insurance Law — **Act No. 2427**
- public utilities legislation

### Agabin takeaway

**PUBLIC / CONSTITUTIONAL / PROCEDURAL / COMMERCIAL = MORE AMERICANIZED**

**PRIVATE CIVIL LAW = MORE SPANISH FOUNDATION + COMMON-LAW MODIFICATIONS**

**Result = mestizo legal system.**

# 3.6 THE QUEST FOR THE “FILIPINO”

Main question:
**After authoritarian concentration of power, how should political authority be reorganized and distributed?**

Flow:
**1973 Constitution → Marcos amendments / concentration → 1986 EDSA → Freedom Constitution → permanent democratic Constitution → decentralization / federalism debate**

## 1973 Constitution + Marcos Amendments

- formal constitutional change
- 1976 / 1981 amendments strengthened presidential/executive dominance
- result discussed in the notes: **highly centralized authoritarian state**

## 1986 Freedom Constitution — Proclamation No. 3

Transitional post-EDSA framework under Corazon Aquino.

- retained important provisions on territory, citizenship, Bill of Rights, suffrage
- removed / superseded Batasang Pambansa and Prime Minister
- legislative authority temporarily vested in the President because the old legislature was dismantled
- priorities:
  - government reorganization
  - human rights
  - recovery of ill-gotten wealth
  - anti-corruption

## Constitutional Commission — Proclamation No. 9

- appointive **50-member** commission
- represented areas / sectors
- tasked to draft permanent Constitution
- commissioners barred from seeking office in the first post-ratification elections

Core logic:
**remove authoritarian structures → transitional executive framework → permanent democratic Constitution**

## Centralization / Decentralization

Colonial centralized structures were discussed as useful for:
- taxation
- resource extraction
- policy enforcement
- political control

Decentralization efforts in the notes:
- Local Autonomy Act of 1959
- Decentralization Act of 1967
- Local Government Code of 1991 — **RA 7160**

Important distinction:
**de jure autonomy ≠ practical / fiscal autonomy**

Also:
**political decentralization ≠ economic decentralization**

## Federalism

- national + regional governments have constitutionally protected spheres
- **dual sovereignty** in their respective domains
- **subsidiarity** = decisions should be made at the **lowest capable level**

### Models Discussed

**USA**
- federal + presidential
- Senate
- separated executive/legislature

**Canada**
- federal + parliamentary
- appointed Senate
- federal/provincial spheres
- accommodation of cultural / multinational differences

**Germany**
- federal + parliamentary
- **Bundesrat** represents Länder/state governments
- strong regional administration

Memory:
**USA = President + elected Senate**

**Canada = Parliament + appointed Senate**

**Germany = Parliament + Bundesrat**

## Presidential vs Parliamentary vs Semi-Presidential

### Presidential criticisms

- **dual legitimacy / gridlock**
- personality / patronage politics
- celebrity / family / media influence
- weak parties / turncoatism
- pork-barrel dynamics
- fixed terms = rigidity

### Parliamentary advantages

- executive-legislative fusion
- stronger policy coordination
- party discipline
- vote of no confidence
- government can be replaced / election may follow

### Semi-presidential

**dual executive:**
- President = popularly elected head of state
- Prime Minister = responsible to parliamentary majority / head of government

Possible problem:
**President + PM from opposing camps → conflict**

### Philippine Federal Proposals

**ABUEVA / CMFP**
- **federal-parliamentary**
- **11 autonomous States**

- Prime Minister
- bicameral Parliament

- presidential government
- bicameral Congress
- equal Senate representation among regions

**[VERIFIED FACT-CHECK] Pimentel III / PDP-Laban:** Senate materials describe a **federal model with a hybrid / semi-presidential executive**, built around **11 regional governments**, with a directly elected President as head of State and a Prime Minister heading government.

## OCT. 3 — PRESIDENTIAL POWERS (PROF'S STUDY GUIDE)

## President as Leader for National Survival

### Emergency / National Security

Congress may authorize the President by law to exercise necessary and proper powers to carry out a declared national policy during war or other national emergency.

Limits:
- Congress prescribes restrictions
- Congress may withdraw the delegation by resolution

### Commander-in-Chief

President may **call out the armed forces** to prevent/suppress:
- lawless violence
- invasion
- rebellion

For **invasion or rebellion**, when public safety requires:
- President may suspend privilege of writ of habeas corpus
- or place the Philippines / part of it under martial law
- **not more than 60 days**
- subject to **congressional review** and **judicial review**

### Emergency control of private business

During a national emergency, when public interest requires, the State may temporarily take over or direct operation of privately owned public utilities or businesses affected with public interest.

---

## President as Economic Leader

- may contract / guarantee foreign loans for the Republic
  - prior Monetary Board concurrence
  - statutory limitations
- submits proposed national budget within **30 days** from opening of every regular session
- **line-item veto** on appropriation, revenue, or tariff bills
- may exercise tariff powers delegated by Congress, within statutory limits
- may enter into treaties / international agreements
  - Senate concurrence of at least **2/3 of all Senate members**
- implements constitutional natural-resource policy through agencies / constitutionally permitted agreements
- appoints members of the **Monetary Board**

---

## President as Legislative Gatekeeper

### General veto

Every bill goes to the President.

Veto override:
- **2/3 of all House members**
- **2/3 of all Senate members**
- voting **separately**

### Line-item veto

May veto one or more items in:
- appropriation bill
- revenue bill
- tariff bill

### Budget control

- proposes expenditure items in national budget
- administers appropriations after enactment

### Legislative agenda

- shapes agenda through the **State of the Nation Address**

## CONTROL

**Control** = power to **alter, modify, nullify, or set aside** what a subordinate officer has done in official duties and **substitute the President's judgment**.

It:
- does not depend on statutory implementation
- cannot be withdrawn / substantially limited by ordinary legislation
- extends to executive departments, bureaus, offices, executive officers, including officers appointed by subordinate bodies

When law entrusts a function to an executive officer:
- President may personally perform or direct the performance
- unless Constitution / law requires the President to act personally

### Qualified Political Agency

Department secretaries are the President's **alter egos** in matters within their departments.

Their acts:
- within their authority
- not disapproved / reprobated by President
- are presumptively acts of the President

Limits discussed in the Prof's guide:
1. President retains authority to **confirm, modify, or reverse** secretaries' acts.
2. President cannot delegate functions the Constitution or law requires the President to exercise personally.
3. President cannot control **independent constitutional commissions** in their constitutionally protected functions.

---

## REORGANIZATION

Control = changes **acts** of subordinates.

Reorganization = changes **structure / location / powers / functions** of executive offices.

Authorities in the Prof's guide:
- **P.D. No. 1416 (1978)** — continuing authority to reorganize National Government administrative structure
- **P.D. No. 1772 (1981)** — expanded authority to create, abolish, merge, or modify offices / GOCCs

Reorganization can include:
- transfer offices / functions
- consolidate / merge offices
- abolish offices
- create offices where legally authorized
- transfer personnel / resources
- realign Executive branch structure

Limits:
- must be supported by Constitution, statute, or valid continuing authority
- cannot be used to violate **security of tenure**
- cannot alter offices/functions beyond authority granted by law

**Easy distinction:**
**CONTROL = “I can change what you did.”**

**REORGANIZATION = “I can change the structure of the office.”**

---

## APPOINTING POWER

President appoints:
- heads of executive departments
- ambassadors / public ministers / consuls
- armed forces officers from **colonel / naval captain** upward
- officers whose appointment is vested in President by Constitution
- officers whose appointment is not otherwise provided by law

Other appointments may be subject to **Commission on Appointments** confirmation where required.

### Midnight Appointments

Generally prohibited from **2 months before the next presidential election until the end of the President's term**.

Exceptions discussed:
- Judiciary appointments under strict timeline
- temporary executive appointments when vacancies would prejudice public service / endanger public safety

### Ad Interim Appointments

President may make temporary appointments during legislative recess, subject to constitutional rules on **ad interim** appointments.

- President

- Chief Justice

Why “hyper”?

**Supplemental synthesis from friend's reviewer:**

Because the Philippine state has:

- strong appointment power
- reorganization authority
- emergency powers
- residual executive powers
- symbolic / political centrality of the presidency
